The cascade rule earns its place by fixing parent errors when a child decision has independent evidence, but the gain is concentrated in the hard parent-child cases rather than in every cleaner scenario. The fact-level flags are the main protection against scattered poison because they isolate the specific bad keys without shredding a whole tool, while the cost is some extra false positives in the early learning period. The fast path saves calls when a trusted agent agrees with itself, but it can over-trust a lone good agent and lose accuracy in late attacks where the wrong signal appears after the warm-up window. The all-off ablation is the oracle check: it should collapse back onto D4 base in every cell, and if it does not the defense logic is wrong. The bad news is that late-started attacks still expose the fast path and that slice quarantines can waste some clean work when they over-flag a warehouse. Overall, the three switches are complementary: cascade corrects the structure, flags limit local damage, and the fast path trades redundancy for efficiency without changing the underlying safety story.
